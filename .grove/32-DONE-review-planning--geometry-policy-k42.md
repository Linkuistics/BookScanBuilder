### 1. Medium — "honest flagged" has no stated mechanism for the case that dominates: every page's within-page geometry is unmeasured

**Contract.** `k41:67–75` requires a supported rigid-rotation decision "plus
unchanged/usable flagged fallback", asks the design to define "what is
assessed, what is unknown and why a page is flagged", and forbids treating
"unmeasured nonrigidity" as absence of distortion or a "certified automatic
'correct' status", and forbids inventing thresholds. `k41:76–78` presumes some
pages are *not* flagged ("including one that was not flagged"). The root's
criterion is "review of flagged pages, with adjustment of any page"
(`brief:30–31`).

**Why it is not yet answerable.** No nonrigid measurement exists for any page
(`k41:29–34`), so the honest status of *every* page is "within-page geometry
unverified". Two readings of "flagged" follow and both fail: (a) flag =
unverified, so every page is flagged, the review queue is the whole book, and
"unflagged" cannot exist; (b) flag = some measurable anomaly (low rotation
confidence, ambiguous split, abnormal border), so every page without a trigger
is "unflagged", which a reviewer and any downstream reader will read as "fine"
— exactly the certified-correct status the charter forbids. The charter also
requires a flag *basis* without permitting a threshold, and says nothing about
whether the exported PDF or review record carries a per-page status that lets
"unverified, no anomaly" be told from "verified".

**What would settle it.** The design must separate (i) a universal limitation
statement that applies to every page until nonrigid evidence exists, from
(ii) per-page triage reasons that are each tied to a recorded measurable
signal, and must say what an unflagged page is permitted to claim. k41 can
still invent no threshold; it can state which signals exist, what each can and
cannot show, and that triage ranking is not certification. Today the charter
does not require that separation, so a coherent-looking design could satisfy
every bullet while one of the two bad readings stands.

### 2. Medium — the rotation/preservation claims have no independent measurement basis, and the one instrument class tried here already failed

**Contract.** `k41:106–119` defines the increment's own acceptance as
"supported rotation improvement, unchanged/flagged unsupported cases, border/
split fidelity …", requires "relevant fractional nonidentity sampling
evidence", and says identity/copy proves no sampler fidelity.

**Why it is not yet answerable.** The experiment's only rigid estimator is
Leptonica `pixFindSkew` on a thresholded page (`pg:25–27`), i.e. an automatic
global angle with a confidence. If acceptance of "rotation improvement" is
measured by the same estimator (or a re-run of it on the output), the check is
circular and detects nothing. The independent real-page instrument the tree
tried — blind observers reading addressed cells through `view_image` — failed
all nine known-cell readbacks (`k41:29–34`, `k39:18–21`), so a real-page
numerical ground truth for angle is not available. The charter says neither
what the numerical seam is nor that a known-by-construction fixture is the
only place a numerical rotation claim can be tested. Two further claims are
left implicit: (a) an upright page and a page with a tiny estimated angle must
not be rotated/resampled for estimator noise (otherwise "already-correct pages
are not degraded", `k41:66`, is violated by the very feature), and (b) a global
angle on a page with within-page rotation variation can improve one region
while worsening another (`k41:68–70` says rigid tilt removal "does not resolve"
variation but does not make "not worse in any region" a checked claim).

**What would settle it.** A statement of which claims are numerical (synthetic
pages rotated by a known angle with known resampling, including zero- and
small-angle controls and a deliberately curved-line control) and which are
visual-only on real pages, with the estimator's output never used as its own
ground truth. Controls must be seen to fail on a deliberately wrong subject
before a clean result is credited (`execute.md`, verifying a claim).

### 3. Medium — "design agreement" has no evidence rule and no terminal state when the human is unavailable

**Contract.** `k41:52–55` requires agreeing the product shape and test seams
"with the human before implementation planning"; `k41:168–178` cuts the planner
"once a coherent design agreement exists", and says that if a missing response
prevents agreement, "externalise only that question with a fresh planner on
EVERY outcome" while never retiring an unagreed design or repeating "identical
unanswered questions on an automatic response/planning loop". The root says the
planner is cut "from an agreed artifact" (`brief:64`).

**Why it is not yet answerable.** Three things the same tree already settled
elsewhere are missing here. (1) The evidence rule: k39 required the actual
reply verbatim with its scope and said silence is not a decision
(`k39:40–43`); k41 says "a coherent design agreement exists" and never names a
recorded human reply as the evidence, so a session can read a coherent,
internally reviewed spec as "agreed". (2) The terminal state: k39 said an
unanswered question leaves the leaf live "for the human rather than retiring
it" (`k39:100–104`); k41's only unanswered-reply branch is "externalise the
question with a fresh planner", and a planner whose only job would be to re-ask
the same design question is exactly the loop line 177 forbids. (3) `design` is
an AFK kind in Grove and its spec is the human sign-off point
(`SPEC-FORMAT.md:3–15`), while this tree's history is that AFK sessions record
human replies as unavailable (k33, k35, k37, k39). The charter therefore
needs a stated path for "drafted, not signed".

The same absence reaches the successor increment: the intermediate product's
"own visual acceptance" (`k41:106–119`, `k23:51–53`) depends on human reactions
that have repeatedly been unavailable, and nothing says whether a working slice
with numerical acceptance and a pending reaction may retire, or what
`appearance-cleanup-k22` may read as "accepted" (`k22:30–35`).

**What would settle it.** Carry k39's three rules: the actual reply recorded
verbatim with scope; unavailable/ambiguous is neither approval nor rejection;
and an unsigned design leaves k41 live rather than cutting a planner or
retiring. State the equivalent disposition vocabulary (accepted numerically /
reaction pending / rejected) for the intermediate increment so its consumers
and the planner do not invent one.

### 4. Medium — review and processing state across the two-stage, shell-driven workflow is not named

**Contract.** The root runs one book "in two stages that can be run from the
shell" and requires review changes to appear in the export (`brief:10–12,
30–31`). `k41:76–83` requires adjustment of any page, explicit acceptance/
reversion, "accepted-image identity/versioning and invalidation at the
behaviour level", and `k41:99–105` forbids a *mandatory sidecar* for the input
and requires safe behaviour on interruption of the destination write.

**Why it is not yet answerable.** Automatic processing of a full book and
human review of its flagged pages are separate, long-running activities.
Nothing in k41 says where accepted/reverted adjustments, flags, post-split
exclusions and movements live between a processing run and an export run, whether
a half-reviewed book can be resumed, what happens if the same PDF is
re-processed after some pages were accepted, or whether that state is a new
persisted artifact. "No mandatory sidecar" is stated for input and could be
misread as ruling out the one thing a review workflow needs. The
prepared-page-hints ADR already shows that anything persisted into a user's PDF
binds later increments (`docs/adr/prepared-page-hints.md`: "persists in the
user's exported PDFs, so changing its meaning requires continued reader
compatibility"); a review-state convention is the same class of decision.

**What would settle it.** The design charter should name the review-state
lifecycle (persistence, resume, re-run invalidation, location relative to the
sole-PDF boundary) as a required answer, and say whether any of it is embedded
in the exported PDF, which would have to be weighed against that ADR.
