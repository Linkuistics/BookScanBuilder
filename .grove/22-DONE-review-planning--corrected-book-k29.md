### 1. High — the "blind" repeat and the "inspection" are not independent of the marker, so k27 can reproduce k26's non-calibrating measurement and still report "measured uncertainty"

**Contract.** `k27:69–76` requires a blind whole-trace repeat "without displaying
first marks, coordinate lists or derived overlays", records uncertainty, and
says only that a same-observer repeat "is not independent ground truth".
`k27:77–81` has the same session inspect its own overlays; `k27:100–103` adds a
human reaction to "the shown native patches". `k27:79–80`: unverified points
"remain ineligible".

**Defect.** k26 recorded precisely this limitation as the reason its repeat could
not calibrate: same-observer repeats "expose failure but cannot establish
independent ground truth or adequate trial uncertainty" (`k26:78–81`;
`note:58–60`, "memory and shared systematic bias cannot be ruled out"). k27
repeats the identical protocol with the identical kind of observer and adds no
route out. Three concrete gaps:
(a) A session that wrote the first-pass coordinates holds them in its working
context; "without displaying" only controls what is re-shown. The session must
also read the frozen record to recover units and rectangles (`k27:58–68`), which
puts the rejected y-values in context while it is told to start a "genuinely
fresh" pass (`k27:51–57`).
(b) Only the repeatability half of "systematic error" is measured. Nothing gives
a truth reference for bias: no synthetic known-centre patches, no labelled
cross-check on unobscured columns. Two passes by one observer agreeing is
precision, not accuracy, and k26's own data shows the observer's coordinates
could be several pixels from the truth.
(c) "Verified" is undefined in coverage. k27 does not say who verifies each of
roughly four hundred points. The marker's overlay inspection is the marker's own
check; the human question shows "the shown native patches", which cannot cover
every rule. So most points' verification is either self-inspection or sampled,
and the same human response has been unavailable four times running (k4, k5,
k20/k26's "Not inspected yet", the pending overlay question).

**Consequence.** k27 can retire with a number labelled "measured uncertainty"
that cannot do what k24 finding 3 and k25 needed it for, resolve a rigid-versus-
traced difference of order one pixel against marking noise. Alternatively it
lands in "unresolved measurement" again, one session later, with nothing new
learned about how to resolve it. Either way k28 receives no more calibration
than k26 handed k21.

**Smallest correction.** State which parts of the protocol must run in a context
that never received first-pass coordinates (the allowed decomposition at
`k27:128–132` is the natural mechanism; today it is framed only as session-size
relief), hand the marker a roster of unit ids, columns and rectangles with the
rejected y-values stripped, and declare what verifies each point (all of it, or a
declared sample with the rest ineligible). Say explicitly what a same-context
agreement may and may not be called, and decide whether a labelled independent
reference (synthetic known-centre patches, or a non-observational centroid
cross-check kept separate from the observations) is admissible as validation;
k20's ban (`.grove/20-k20/_staff-guides.md:84–87`) is on masquerading automatic
extraction as manual tracing, not on checking the marker against truth.

### 2. Medium — no falsifier ties "admissible acquisition" to the resolution the next trial needs; a repeat disagreement of k26's size has no outcome

**Contract.** `k27:42–50` forbids raising the ±3-pixel assertion and lists
rejection only for invented, obscured or outside-rule points; "unmeasurable
repeatability" is inconclusive. `k27:91–99` lists the outcome classes.

**Defect.** The hypothesis is that exact addressing removes the k26 errors
(1.0/4.0/6.0/4.0/1.5/2.5 px maxima, `note:66–73`). The plan says nothing about
what result would count as failing it. A repeat that is *measurable* but as
coarse as k26's, with all points genuinely on rules in the overlays, fits no
named class: it is not invalid, not unmeasurable, not a coverage failure. The
likeliest reading is "admissible, with a large measured uncertainty", which is
the renamed repeat the review charter asks about. The charter also gives k28 no
source-only quantity to weigh "warrants the trial" against: the instrument
already computes per-rule rise and curvature (`inst:146–155`), but k27 does not
require those to be reported beside the uncertainty. k4's recorded rigid
residuals on page 140 (rises 2.6/1.6/−2.4/−0.2 px, curvature 0.6–1.3 px,
`docs/experiments/page-geometry-report.json`) show the effect scale in this
family can be about a pixel; page 20's is unknown, which is the point.

**Consequence.** The matched automatic/manual trial, three human views and the
output/control/sampler measurements can be commissioned on a page whose
traceable distortion is below the acquisition noise, producing the
"indistinguishable from measured uncertainty" inconclusive that k25 already
named as the thing not to spend a trial on.

**Smallest correction.** Before marking, state in k27 what repeat-disagreement
pattern, expressed against k26's recorded table rather than as an invented
threshold, falsifies the hypothesis; add a named outcome "repeatable but too
coarse for the effect to be resolved"; require the per-rule rise/curvature of
the final marks to be reported beside the measured uncertainty. Also make the
admissibility decision derived from recorded per-point/per-rule verification
rather than the single hand-set boolean the instrument reads
(`inst:353, 377`), or say that the hand-set flag is only allowed after the
verification record exists.
