### 1. High — the masked native prerequisite is already decided for the right half and nearly so for the left; k35 orders it last and forbids the repair

**Contract.** `k35:94–110`: freeze whole-line/region holdouts before extraction;
extract only "if independently verified acquisition warrants it" on the masked
halves with unchanged threshold, .8 filter and minimum 15; an invalid point or a
failed prerequisite may not "move a holdout, shorten a line"; `k35:48–54`: do
not choose lines "from automatic centres or gate results"; `k35:28–29` and
`exp:68–71`: unmasked counts are "neither masked gates nor bounds", and "the
right's failure does not establish failure after the eventual declared holdouts".

**Defect.** The unmasked counts are an upper bound, not a diagnostic with no
direction. Masking removes ink; it cannot lengthen a text-line component
(`nat:824–856`: closings and openings are horizontal-only, 1-pixel-tall
elements, so removing ink cannot merge lines). Length is `maxx − minx + 1`, the
cut is `0.8 × longest`. From the archived extents:
(a) **Right half, printed 37.** 10 lines pass of the required 15. Sorted lengths
are 1379, 1370, 1337, 1329, 1321, 1303, 1228, 1181, 1164, 1123, 1012, 932, 896,
864, 838, 757… The fifteenth is 838, so fifteen retained lines need the longest
surviving line ≤ 1047 (0.8 × 1047 = 837.6). Ten lines are longer than that.
Whole-line holdouts only remove lines (10 → 9 → … → 3 as the longest are
removed). A full-height gutter-side band mask of width 0–400 px leaves 10; wider
is worse (9, 8). Only a selective cap of exactly the ten longest lines at ≤1047
reaches 15 (1048 gives 14): a mask pattern read off the gate result, which
`k35:48–54` and `k35:94–100` forbid. So the right half's masked native
prerequisite is not an open question.
(b) **Left half, printed 36.** Exactly 15 retained, the minimum. The fifteenth
retained line is 1070 px against a 1063.2 cut; the next is 899. Removing any one
retained line gives 14, so **a whole-line holdout among the retained lines
fails by construction**. A full-height band mask over the gutter end of 0–385 px
keeps 15 (X ≥ 1400 in half coordinates), and the sixteenth appears only at
X ≤ 1350, so region holdouts can pass while whole-line ones cannot. The
non-retained left lines end at x ≤ 1398 and none reaches the gutter ink the crops
begin at x=1485, so holdouts confined to non-retained lines cannot supply
"actual gutter" extent either (`k35:94–100`). k35 never says which holdout type
it expects.
(c) The prose control retains 35 against 15, so headroom exists where the page
has full-width lines; Norwegian's short-line layout is what is binding.
Caveat: this is first-order arithmetic on the committed extraction. It ignores
removal of tall (≥50-px vertical run) components at `nat:840–845`, which a mask
could in principle un-merge into extra lines; it also relies on the archive's
extents, not a masked run, which this review may not make.

**Consequence.** k35 spends both halves' complete acquisition (fresh first marks,
blind whole-line repeats, per-point verification, frozen holdouts, observer
delegation) before the one cheap, mark-independent step that can decide each
half's native outcome. The likeliest result is "inadequate native prerequisite"
for the right half (and for the left unless the frozen holdouts happen to be
band-type), after which `k35:109–118` bars repair. The first k31 question,
whether the automatic vertical candidate improves each half beyond rigid, could
then never reach a comparison on this fixture, and the planner's "exact changed
observation" would be a holdout/gate/fixture decision, not an instrument one,
discovered after the spend. The committed sentence at `exp:70–71` points the
wrong way for the right half and should not seed that planner.

**Smallest correction.** Make a mark-independent feasibility step the first act
of k35, before dense marking: state the holdout shape the contract allows
(region or whole-line), derive from the committed unmasked extents whether any
admissible holdout leaves ≥15 retained lines, ≥4 per half-height, span >.50 and
gutter-end coverage, and run the masked extraction on the analysis copy once the
holdouts are frozen from physical regions and not from gate output. A half that
cannot meet the unchanged native prerequisite is recorded as a decisive
prerequisite outcome with its numbers, and marking effort on it stops unless the
human wants instrument evidence there. Say in k35 and k28 what the planner may
propose for such a half (fixture, holdout concept or gate are human decisions, per
k25's pool rule) and that `exp:70–71` no longer stands for the right half.

### 2. Medium — addressing, the hinge of the changed observation, is an optional control placed beside the marks

**Contract.** `k35:55–62`: patches carry a grid/readout that "must resolve a
selected source pixel reproducibly"; known-centre synthetic controls "may check
coordinate addressing and observer error separately". `k35:111–120` lists
"unavailable instrument" first.

**Defect.** The one thing that differs from k33 is that coordinates are now
drawn on the patch and read back by a model through the same image viewer. That
is a rendering change, and whether an observer reads a 1:1 or integer-zoom grid
to ±1 px is exactly the open instrument question. The plan makes that check
optional ("may"), does not say it precedes first marks, and gives no outcome for
"observers misread the grid by more than the glyph-bottom features being
measured", only the generic "unavailable instrument". No threshold may be invented
(k35 is right about that), but a recorded known-centre error distribution on the
same patch format, scale and observer class is the one observation that is cheap,
unambiguous and real.

**Consequence.** The slice can reach full marking, repeats and verification on an
addressing method it never tested, then classify the failure as "unresolved
systematic uncertainty" or "ambiguous", which is the same indeterminate k33
outcome with more sessions behind it.

**Smallest correction.** Require the known-centre addressing check, on the exact
patch format and observer class used for the real marks, before first marks, with
its observed error recorded and a named stop if grid readings cannot be
reproduced; keep stating it calibrates addressing only.

### 3. Medium — "shared systematic limits" have no reference the plan admits, and a source property that would bias slope is not carried into k35

**Contract.** `k35:85–93`: state and justify shared systematic limits from
"glyph anchoring/display/addressing", separately from repeatability; agreement of
fresh same-model observers "is not ground truth"; `k35:40–47, 58–64`: synthetic
controls "establish no real-page accuracy"; `k35:77–85`: a sampled human check
cannot verify the rest; `k35:121–126`: the human reaction.

**Defect.** The bias that matters for slope and curvature is the position-
dependent part of the baseline offset (a constant offset cancels). The plan's
three references cannot identify it: same-model repeats and verifiers share it,
synthetic patches do not carry real blur and overshoot by its own statement, and
the human check is a sample whose reply has been unavailable at every request
(`exp:192–198`; k4 and k21's "Not inspected yet"). What *is* observable inside
the real page, with no outside truth, is the offset between anchor classes along
the same line (flat-footed stems and serif feet against round bottoms, upright
against italic), because the glyph class changes with x. `k35:70–76` asks each
anchor to be justified individually and does not require a predeclared anchor
class, a per-class offset, or stratified anchors per line. Separately, the
source is a 220-ppi grayscale JPEG (`pdfimages -list`: 2633×1976, 220×220 ppi,
jpeg, page 20) resampled to 300 dpi (`exp:29–31`); "native 300-dpi pixel"
addressing is below the scan's own sample spacing and the JPEG block grid
(8 px × 300/220 ≈ 10.9 px period) is a position-dependent artefact. Neither
appears among k35's systematic sources.

**Consequence.** "Defensible shared systematic limits" can only be the author's
argument, with no rule for when it suffices, so the classification between
"unresolved systematic uncertainty" and "admissible" is left to the producing
session's judgement. A class-dependent offset or block artefact comparable to
the nonrigidity would be learned only after output work.

**Smallest correction.** Predeclare the anchor classes, require several classes
per line where the text offers them, report the within-line per-class offset as
the internal systematic estimate beside repeatability, add resampling and block
grid to the listed sources, and say which components (constant versus
position-dependent) the stop decision compares with observed nonrigidity.
